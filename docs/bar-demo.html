What this is

A drop-in replacement for the rainfall strip in the site header. It reads the same LCRA Hydromet gauges as the full dashboard and uses the same 48-hour definition: yesterday's full-day total plus today's rain since midnight.

The design follows the site's own patterns rather than the strip it replaces: a white card with a gold eyebrow over a heavy headline, readings laid out as the stat grid used elsewhere on the site, hairline rules between them, and the gold call-to-action with its arrow nested in a blue box. Gold marks the wettest location — but only when it has actually rained, since highlighting a five-way tie at zero would be noise.

To embed

Paste this into a Webflow Embed element (or any raw-HTML block). Nothing else is needed — no libraries, no build step.

<div id="drw-rainfall-bar"
     data-dashboard="https://cawthorneh.github.io/projects/"></div>
<script src="https://cawthorneh.github.io/projects/rainfall-bar.js"></script>

The data-dashboard attribute is optional; when set, the "Rainfall Monitor" label links through to the full dashboard. If you'd rather not depend on a hosted file, paste the contents of rainfall-bar.js inline inside a <script> tag instead — it renders where it is pasted.

When a gauge has no reading

The cell shows a dash, not 0.00", and the caption says readings are unavailable. Zero means it did not rain, which is a different claim from not knowing — and the strip on the site today shows 0.00" everywhere, which is what that failure looks like when the two are conflated.

On small screens

The readings reflow to three columns, then two — all five stay visible without scrolling, rather than hiding behind a swipe.
